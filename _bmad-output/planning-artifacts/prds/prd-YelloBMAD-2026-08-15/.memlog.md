- (change) UPDATE INTENT 2026-08-20, escalated from the bmad-ux run. DEFECT: the Ownership Offer had an expiry and no notification. FR-8's assumption said the offer is 'surfaced in Space settings rather than emailed - the recipient is already a Member of the Space', which is true and insufficient: being a Membership of a Space is not the same as being present in it. Composed with the 7-day expiry and with expiry evaluated only on read (AD-27), a recipient who does not open that Space for a week never learns the offer existed. UJ-8's lapse edge case then bites the wrong person - the offer lapses, the offering Owner remains Owner, FR-14 still forbids removing their Membership and FR-3 still refuses their Account deletion, so an Owner can be held in a Space indefinitely because somebody else never logged in, with deleting the Space as their only unilateral exit. The asymmetry made it plainer: FR-40 emails you for a single Task assignment; nothing emailed you when offered an entire Space. CHANGES: FR-8 assumption reduced to the expiry alone, with a new consequence that the recipient is told, plus a 'Why the recipient is emailed' note preserving the retired reasoning so a later reader sees what changed. NEW FR-43 in section 4.11 delivering the offer by email, carrying an explicit disclosure constraint mirroring FR-39 - names the Space, the ownership offer and who offered it, and nothing about the Space's contents, other Memberships or any other Space. That clause is a stated consequence rather than an implementation detail because NFR-1 binds notifications and an email already delivered cannot be recalled when the offer is revoked or lapses. Also: the email carries NO means of acceptance (FR-42 keeps acceptance inside the Space), and exactly one email per offer.
- (decision) CONFLICT SURFACED AND RESOLVED IN-DOCUMENT rather than blocking: adding FR-43 runs against SM-C4, which names notification volume as a counter-metric. Resolution recorded as a 'Why this does not violate SM-C4' note under FR-43. SM-C4's wording is narrower than it first reads - it guards against volume that 'should not increase to drive SM-3 or SM-4', i.e. notifications added to chase adoption or invitation conversion. FR-43 drives no metric; it removes a reachable trap. One email per offer against at most one pending offer per Space (FR-8) is the smallest volume that closes it. Stated in the PRD rather than only here, so a later reader does not read FR-43 as a counter-metric violation someone missed.
