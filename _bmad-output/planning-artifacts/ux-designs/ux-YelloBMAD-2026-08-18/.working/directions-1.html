Yello — design directions on Dusk

The palette is fixed: direction E — Dusk, shown dark because you chose the dark-first option. These four vary only what is still open, applied to the same hero screen so the comparison is personality rather than colour.

Hero is the Board — a view of the Project “Website Rebuild” in the Space “Northwind Redesign”, columns by Status. Each direction also shows the two states that constrain it hardest: the same Board as a Viewer, and an empty Project.

What varies, and why each axis is load-bearing rather than taste
DensityFR-28 requires the Board usable at 5,000 Tasks in a Project while still meeting NFR-5 and NFR-9. The PRD says plainly the three cannot all hold naively. Roomier cards mean fewer visible per screen.
Type & weightTask titles are the only content on a card. Everything else is metadata. How much the title dominates decides whether the Board scans or reads.
Shape & elevationDusk carries a tinted ground, so shadow separation is weaker than it would be on grey. Radius and border weight are what actually hold the card apart from the column.
Motion implicationYou adopted motion as a concern. A dense systematic surface implies short, flat transitions; a roomy one implies longer, softer settle. The direction sets the timing budget, so it is chosen here rather than later.

1 — Compact

Tight and systematic: small uppercase column heads, 12.5px card titles, 5px between cards. Roughly twice the Tasks on screen as direction 2, which is the direct answer to FR-28’s 5,000-Task bound — the fewer virtualised rows you need, the less of NFR-9’s keyboard reachability problem you inherit. The cost is that it reads as a control panel; there is no warmth here and long Task titles get cramped.

radius 6 · card pad 7/9 · gap 5 · title 12.5px/500 · col head 11px/700 uppercase · meta 10px · elevation 1px hairline shadow · motion implied: 100–140ms, flat easing

2 — Roomy

Generous and humane: 15px titles at a light weight, 14px radius, real breathing room between cards. The most pleasant of the four to sit in for an hour and the one that best matches a product whose success condition is a calm exit rather than a long session. The cost is direct and measurable — roughly half the Tasks per screen, which makes FR-28’s 5,000-Task Board a harder virtualisation problem and more scrolling for everyone.

radius 14 · card pad 14/15 · gap 10 · title 15px/450 · col head 12.5px/600 sentence case · meta 11.5px · elevation 2/8 soft · motion implied: 180–240ms, eased settle

3 — Editorial

The chrome recedes and type does the work: Task titles set in a serif at 16px, cards reduced to rows separated by a hairline rule rather than boxed and floated. Sidesteps Dusk’s weakest property — shadows separate poorly on a tinted ground, so this direction stops relying on them. The risk is that a Task stops reading as a draggable object; a rule-separated row invites a click, not a lift, which fights the long-press gesture you chose.

radius 5 · card pad 11/4 · rule separators, no shadow · title 16px/400 serif · col head 11px/700 wide uppercase · meta 10.5px sans · motion implied: 140–180ms, opacity-led

4 — Engineered

Hard 3px corners, brighter borders, no shadow at all, and every piece of metadata — counts, Labels, initials, the Role chip — set in monospace. Structure is drawn rather than floated, which is the most robust choice at density and the one that survives Dusk’s tinted ground best. It also speaks directly to who this is for: Ravi is a freelance developer and Tomás drives Yello from a deploy script. The risk is that it signals “developer tool” loudly enough to put off Beatriz, the client Viewer who never asked for one.

radius 3 · card pad 9/10 · gap 6 · title 13px/600 sans · all metadata monospace · borders #33406B, no shadow · square-ish avatars · motion implied: 90–120ms, near-instant
