# Accessibility review — Yello UX spine pair

## Overall verdict

**Not gate-ready.** The spine pair is unusually accessibility-literate for a design document — the focus-ring/accent token separation, Presence-as-dot-plus-text, the per-script uppercase fallback, the border raised to clear 1.4.11, and the explicit refusal to let the long-press gesture discharge NFR-9 are all decisions most design specs get wrong, and they were got right here on purpose. But the compliance argument rests in three places on statements that do not survive arithmetic or implementation: the keyboard path is specified in a key grammar that a screen-reader user in browse mode cannot reach at all, the FR-34 interruption destroys the focused node with no specified focus destination, and there is no single-pointer alternative to the Board drag on a touch tablet — a **Level A** failure, not AA.

Separately, the FR-28 × NFR-5 × NFR-9 collision that the PRD flagged as unable to "all hold naively" is owned by nobody: the PRD defers it to architecture, the architecture spine's 28 ADs never decide it, and `EXPERIENCE.md` defers back to "the architecture's call". Virtualisation is therefore both undesigned and load-bearing for the gate.

The contrast table itself is sound in direction — every dark figure is **understated**, so nothing passes on a wrong number. Two light-theme figures are overstated, and the token the document warns about ("little headroom — do not darken") is not the token that actually has no headroom.

## 1. Contrast arithmetic

**Verdict: arithmetically safe, rhetorically misdirected, and materially incomplete.** No claimed figure creates a false pass. But the table covers 12 combinations out of roughly 40 the design implies, states **zero** light-theme text ratios for a theme that is a runtime peer, and points its one warning at the wrong token.

- **[high]** The light-theme border has 2% headroom over the gate and is described as "clear". `{colors.border-hairline-light}` on `{colors.surface-column-light}` computes to **3.06:1** against 1.4.11's 3:1 (Colors → *Verified contrast*, final paragraph: "Both clear."). Meanwhile `{colors.accent}` on card — which the table flags "passes, with little headroom — do not darken" — computes to **4.96:1**, 10% clear of 4.5. The warning is on the token with five times the margin. *Fix:* move the "do not touch" annotation to `border-hairline-light`, and raise it to ≥3.3:1 (roughly `#727F9E`) so it holds a real margin on all three light grounds.
- **[high]** Every border pair in both themes drops below 3:1 under partial pixel coverage, and one component guarantees partial coverage. A 1px border is rasterised with antialiasing whenever its edge lands off a device-pixel boundary — which a **3px** spacing grid does at the Windows/Android display scales of 1.25×, 1.5× and 1.75× (3px CSS = 3.75/4.5/5.25 device px). At 80% coverage: light-on-column **2.37**, light-on-card **2.62**, light-on-page **2.49**, dark-on-card **2.83**, dark-on-column **2.99**. All fail. And `{components.task-card-lifted}` applies `transform: rotate(-1deg)`, which antialiases its border along its entire length *unconditionally* — so the one object whose boundary matters most (the Task in your hand) is the one guaranteed to render below 3:1. *Fix:* specify the structural border at **1.5px** minimum, or state that borders must be device-pixel-snapped; and give `task-card-lifted` a 2px border for the duration of the drag.
- **[high]** The Label colour constraint is stated against one theme only, and the same user-chosen colour renders on both. Components → Label chip requires a Label fill "must hold 3:1 against `{colors.surface-card}`" — the **dark** card, `#18213C`. There is no light-theme Label token and no instruction to derive one, so a fill satisfying 3:1 against a near-black ground will routinely fail 3:1 against `#FFFFFF` (the two grounds are 17.35:1 apart). This is a whole class of user-generatable 1.4.11 failures in a release-gated product. *Fix:* constrain Label fills to hold 3:1 against **both** `surface-card` and `surface-card-light` simultaneously — which the "constrained palette rather than a free picker" decision already makes cheap to enforce.
- **[high]** Accent text links fail 1.4.1 as specified. Components → Buttons: "if an action does not merit one of these two it is a text link in `{colors.accent}`." No underline or other non-colour distinction is mandated anywhere in either spine. `accent` vs `text-primary` = **2.66:1**; `accent-light` vs `text-primary-light` = **2.55:1**. SC 1.4.1's accepted technique for colour-only link differentiation requires ≥3:1 against surrounding text *plus* a non-colour cue on hover and focus. Both themes miss the 3:1, and no cue exists. *Fix:* underline text links, or state that they always carry a non-colour affordance. Cheapest correct answer for this design: underline on all inline links, since the register is "engineered" and an underline is not decoration.
- **[medium]** The Role chip fill is invisible against its own ground — in both themes. `{components.role-chip}` sets `background: {colors.role-chip}` and **no border**; the context bar it sits in is `{colors.surface-card}`. `role-chip` on `surface-card` = **1.05:1**; light, `role-chip-light` on `surface-card-light` = **1.29:1**. The chip will not read as a chip; it reads as bare mono text. Not a hard 1.4.11 breach (EXPERIENCE.md makes it explicitly non-interactive, and the text carries the content at 8.56:1) — but UJ-4's central bet is that the Role reads as a *distinct permanent system fact*, and a 1.05:1 fill does not deliver that for a low-vision user. *Fix:* add `border: 1px solid {colors.border-hairline}` (3.44:1 against the chip fill, so it works), or shift the fill to 3:1 against the card.
- **[medium]** Five components ship with no foreground token, on a document whose stated method is that "the load-bearing combinations are stated rather than assumed". `{components.bulk-move-bar}`, `{components.column-count}`, `{components.label-chip}`, `{components.picker}`, `{components.space-switcher}` and `{components.avatar}` all specify background/border/typography and omit text colour. Each is an unverified pair; each happens to pass if it inherits `text-primary` or `text-muted`, but the table does not cover the components as written. *Fix:* add explicit `foreground` to each, then extend the table.
- **[medium]** The focus ring's stated rationale is arithmetically hollow. Colors: focus-ring is "a separate token from accent, on purpose… if it inherited `{colors.accent}` it would vanish against accented controls". `focus-ring` vs `accent` = **1.45:1** (light **1.48:1**) — it *would* vanish against an accented control. What actually saves it is `outline-offset: 2px` putting the ring on the ground behind (7.17:1 on card). So the protection is the offset, not the token separation, and the Do/Don't table guards the wrong thing. *Fix:* keep both tokens, but add the real rule — "never reduce `outline-offset` to 0 and never draw the ring inset" — because that is the property compliance depends on.
- **[medium]** `{colors.accent}` and `{colors.danger}` are indistinguishable by luminance, and DESIGN.md asserts the border is the sole signal. `accent` vs `danger` = **1.19:1** dark, **1.08:1** light — the two differ by hue alone. Yet Components → Destructive confirm says "The border is the signal; there is no icon and no illustration", and description-editor-inert says "The border is what carries 'this is over'". Under deuteranopia the accent-bordered bulk-move bar and the danger-bordered destructive confirm converge. Compliance survives *only* because `EXPERIENCE.md` supplies copy ("This cannot be undone.", "Access ended.") — but a builder reading `DESIGN.md` alone would ship a colour-only signal. *Fix:* correct both sentences to name the copy as the signal and the border as reinforcement.
- **[medium]** The `ΔE 10` Label constraint is unimplementable as written and omits two tokens. Components → Label chip: "may not fall within ΔE 10 of `{colors.focus-ring}` or `{colors.danger}`". No colour space is named — ΔE76 and ΔE2000 differ by roughly a factor of two at these chromas — and the list omits `{colors.accent}` (the Offer indicator and every primary button) and `{colors.presence}`. A Label matching accent is confusable with the one chrome element that is a proposition. *Fix:* specify ΔE2000, and add `accent` and `presence` to the exclusion set.
- **[medium]** The light theme has no stated text ratios at all, and it is the theme with every thin margin. The table's light paragraph covers two border figures and stops. On recomputation the light theme does pass throughout — but its slimmest pairs are all undocumented: `presence-light` on `surface-column-light` **4.61:1** (2.4% over 4.5), `focus-ring-light` on `surface-column-light` **3.97:1**, `border-hairline-light` on `surface-page-light` **3.28:1** (not stated, though the column border sits on the page ground). And the light tonal ladder is *flatter* than the dark one the document uses to justify the strong border: page→column **1.07:1** light vs **1.10:1** dark. The weaker theme is the unverified one. *Fix:* state the full light table; it is the same twelve rows.
- **[low]** Every dark figure is understated by 0.02–0.61, which reads as hand-rounding rather than computation. Largest: `text-primary` on card claimed 12.6, actual **13.21**. One dark figure is overstated — `accent-on` on `accent` claimed 5.7, actual **5.63**. Harmless in every case, but a table presented as "verified" against a release gate should be reproducible. *Fix:* regenerate the table from the hex values and note the formula used.
- **Handled well, briefly:** the two combinations the audit brief flagged as suspect are fine. `{colors.text-muted}` on card is **6.53:1** (claimed 6.2 — understated), and the description editor, which correctly sits on `surface-page` rather than the card, gives `text-primary` **15.73:1**, `border-hairline` **4.32:1**, and in the inert state `danger` **7.03:1** with `text-muted` **7.78:1**. The recessed writing surface is the *best*-contrasting ground in the product. `role-chip-on` on `role-chip` is **8.56:1** at 9px, and `danger-on` on `danger` **6.29:1**. The decision recorded in `.memlog.md` entry 35 — raising the border and paying the "gridded" cost in the design rather than at the audit — was the right call and is the reason this section is a set of refinements rather than a rebuild.

## 2. The three input paths

**Verdict: the weakest area, and the one that fails the gate outright.** The keyboard path is complete enough for a *sighted* keyboard user and substantially inoperable for a screen-reader user, which inverts the population NFR-9 exists to protect. The touch path has a Level A failure and a scroll-intent rule that does not survive the document's own breakpoints.

- **[critical]** No single-pointer alternative to the Board drag at 768–1279px. SC **2.5.1 Pointer Gestures is Level A**: all functionality using a path-based gesture must also be operable with a single pointer without a path. Interaction Primitives gives the touch user only "long-press to lift, then drag" — path-based. Responsive & Platform supplies the alternative ("moving between Statuses uses the Task's Status control, not a gesture") **only below 768px**. A touch tablet at 768–1279px therefore has no non-path route to a cross-column move, and the keyboard path is not an alternative for a device with no keyboard. This is a Level A failure inside the Board, one of NFR-9's five named flows. *Fix:* make the Task's Status control the universal cross-column alternative at **every** breakpoint. It already exists, FR-29 already defines Status-change-as-move ("Moving a Task to a column sets its Status"), and this also pre-satisfies WCAG 2.2's 2.5.7 Dragging Movements.
- **[critical]** The entire keyboard grammar is unreachable in screen-reader browse mode, and the spec does not acknowledge the mode exists. Interaction Primitives binds `↑ ↓ ← →` to Task and column traversal and `Space` to pick-up. In NVDA's and JAWS's default browse/virtual mode, arrow keys move the *virtual cursor* through the buffer and `Space`/`Enter` activate — the application never receives those keys. So a screen-reader user cannot pick up a Task, cannot move it, and cannot cancel. Nothing in either spine mentions application mode, `role="application"`, forms mode, or an alternative. NFR-9's "every Board operation available by pointer is available by keyboard" is met for a sighted keyboard user and not met for the user the requirement is written for. *Fix:* do not try to rescue the arrow grammar with `role="application"` — that suppresses the user's own navigation and is a known trap. Add an explicit **"Move to…" affordance** on each Task, reachable by `Enter`, opening a picker of destination column + position. It survives browse mode, needs no mode switching, collapses toward the `<768px` Status-control path already specified, and is the same control that fixes the 2.5.1 failure above.
- **[high]** `Space` is globally rebound without being scoped, colliding with native activation and page scroll. `Space` is the native activation key for every button, and the native page-down for a scroll container — and Board columns are scroll containers ("Columns scroll within the column"). The keyboard list binds `Enter` to "open the focused Task" and `Space` to "pick up". So on a focused control in a column head (the create affordance, the bulk-move entry point), does `Space` activate the button or pick up a Task? The spec gives no scope. *Fix:* state that the pick-up binding applies only when a Task card holds focus, and that `Space` retains native activation on every other control.
- **[high]** `← →` "preserving position where possible" is three different products. Columns hold different counts, and at 5,000 Tasks one may hold 4,000 beside a neighbour holding 3. Clamp to last index? Nearest proportional position? Remember the origin index for the duration of a traversal run? All three are plausible readings of "where possible", they diverge sharply, and no keyboard-traversal test can be written against the phrase. *Fix:* pick one and name it. Sticky-origin-index with clamping is the conventional answer and the least surprising.
- **[high]** The keyboard drop has no specified destination position. When a carried Task enters a column via `← →`, does it land at the top, at the same ordinal, at the nearest position, or at the end? The pointer path has an answer (the drop zone follows the cursor); the keyboard path has none — and AD-15 computes a **jittered fractional index between two neighbours**, so the implementation needs a concrete neighbour pair the spec never supplies. This is the exact point where the keyboard path must produce a persisted value, and it is blank. *Fix:* specify that a carried Task entering a column lands at the same ordinal clamped to the column length, and that `↑ ↓` then adjusts before the `Space` commit.
- **[high]** The live-region announcement for the keyboard drag is promised but its content is unspecified — and it is doing all the work. "Every pick-up, move and drop **announces via an ARIA live region**." For a blind user this announcement *replaces* the entire visual drop-zone system, so it must carry destination column name, position ordinal and column total; without an ordinal the user has no idea where the Task went, and without a total they cannot tell they are at the end. One unspecified sentence is carrying the whole accessible path. *Fix:* specify the string shape — "Northwind: moved to In Progress, position 3 of 12" — and specify that a cancel announces the restoration ("returned to Todo, position 7").
- **[high]** The long-press scroll-intent rule has no slop radius and covers only one axis. "**Vertical** movement before the threshold is a scroll, and cancels the pending lift." Read literally, 1px of finger jitter cancels every lift — no dead zone is stated, and every real implementation needs one (8–10px is conventional). Worse, the rule is single-axis while the document itself creates a horizontal scroll: Responsive & Platform, 768–1279px, "Columns side by side with **horizontal Board scroll**". At that breakpoint a horizontal swipe to pan the Board is indistinguishable from the start of a cross-column drag, and the rule says nothing about it. *Fix:* state a slop radius and make the rule axis-agnostic — movement beyond N px in *any* direction before the threshold is a pan and cancels the lift.
- **[high]** 320ms is a motor threshold with no accommodation, and combined with the zero-slop rule it is systematically unreachable for a tremor user. 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable does not bite (a gesture threshold is not a time limit on reading or responding), so this is not a named SC failure — but a user with essential tremor cannot hold still for 320ms, every incidental movement is reclassified as scroll, and the lift is therefore not merely difficult but unreachable. `.memlog.md` entry 21 records that this path was chosen knowing it is "the most fragile of the four" and that tap-to-select-then-place is the named fallback — which is the correct mitigation and is also the fix for the two criticals above. *Fix:* ship the "Move to…" affordance now rather than as a contingency; it converts three of this section's findings into one control.
- **[high]** The bulk move has no keyboard or focus story, and the only cancel route is a transient bar. Component Patterns: the bulk-move bar "is the only place the operation can be cancelled before commit". State Patterns: the in-flight state "Blocks interaction on the affected columns only." So a bar appears somewhere on the Board and simultaneously the region that may hold focus is blocked. Nothing specifies that focus moves to the bar, nor that the bar is announced with its cancel affordance. A keyboard user who initiates a 4,812-Task move can lose the only route to cancelling it. *Fix:* move focus to the bulk-move bar on appearance, give it `role="status"` for the scope line, and specify where focus goes on commit and on cancel.
- **[medium]** `Esc` carries three meanings and two of them collide on one surface. "`Esc` — close the topmost dialog, cancel a pick-up, leave an editor." The description editor lives inside the Task detail panel, which is a one-level-deep dialog. Pressing `Esc` in the editor: leave the editor, or close the panel? No precedence is given, and for an AT user that is the difference between exiting a textarea and losing the panel and its unsaved text. *Fix:* state the precedence — innermost meaning wins, so first `Esc` leaves the editor and a second closes the panel.
- **[medium]** The `<768px` path does not say what happens to a carried Task. At that breakpoint one column is visible with a Status pager, and the cross-column gesture is removed. Is the `Space` + `← →` grammar still live? If so, a carried Task moves into a column that is off-screen — with no specified announcement, no pager advance, and no visible drop zone. *Fix:* state that at `<768px` the keyboard path uses the same "Move to…" picker as touch, and that the pager follows a committed move.
- **Handled well, briefly:** reordering *within* a column is genuinely complete on the keyboard path (`Space` to pick up, `↑ ↓` within one, `Space` to drop) — the audit brief asked and the answer is yes. **2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation** is satisfied explicitly and correctly ("drag back to origin, or release outside any drop zone, and nothing moves"). And the spine's refusal to let the gesture discharge the requirement — "**This gesture does not discharge NFR-9**" — is the single most important sentence in the section and is exactly right.

## 3. Live regions

**Verdict: the politeness split is correct and the mechanism is not.** `assertive` is the right politeness for a remote permission change and the wrong *tool* for it, because the event destroys the focused node and `assertive` does not move focus. Storms are unmitigated at every scale bound, and the Board's own mutations are undecided.

- **[critical]** `assertive` announces the FR-34 interruption and leaves the user stranded. `aria-live="assertive"` interrupts the current utterance; it does **not** move focus. The FR-34 event makes the element that currently holds focus inert (removal) or **replaces it with rendered text** (demotion — UJ-6 variant: "The editor is replaced by rendered text"). Replacing a focused node removes it from the DOM, so focus resets to `document.body` in every browser and the virtual cursor position is lost. The user hears "Access ended", then finds themselves nowhere, in a surface whose write affordances have all just become *absent* per the Role-legibility rule, with their retained text having no specified place in the reading order. The PRD calls FR-34 "the acceptance criterion this product should be judged on"; for a screen-reader user it currently lands as an unexplained collapse. *Fix:* make the banner `role="alertdialog"` (or `role="alert"` + `tabindex="-1"`) and **move focus to it**, with the retained text as the next stop in the reading order and an explicit statement that it was not saved.
- **[high]** Presence announcement storms are guaranteed rather than prevented, and `polite` is what guarantees them. NFR-8 permits **10 concurrent editors per Task**; NFR-3 requires Presence to appear within 2s of arrival and disappear within 10s of departure, both without the participant acting. Ten editors cycling produces up to 20 discrete transitions in a few seconds, and `polite` **queues** rather than coalesces — so the AT user hears a serialised backlog ("2 editing… 3 editing… 2 editing…") that outlives the events and talks over their own typing echo. No debounce, coalescing, rate floor or suppression-while-typing appears anywhere in either spine. *Fix:* announce the settled count rather than each transition, debounce to ~5s, and suppress Presence announcements entirely while the user is typing in the editor.
- **[high]** The Presence live region is unscoped, so it also carries the whole Board's churn. Component Patterns places the Presence indicator on "Task detail, **Task card**" — so Presence changes on *many cards at once* on the Board. With NFR-8's **50 concurrent Sessions per Space**, aggregate Board Presence churn is unbounded and nothing scopes the region to the open Task. This is worse than the per-Task case because it scales with the Board rather than with the 10-editor bound. *Fix:* announce Presence only for the Task the user has open; render it visually on cards without routing card-level changes to a live region.
- **[high]** Whether Board mutations are announced at all is undecided, and both answers need stating. Remote edits push Tasks into, out of and between columns (AD-9, AD-11), and `DESIGN.md` Motion deliberately makes this silent visually — "**Never animated:** a Task appearing from another User's edit (it arrives, it does not fly in)", which is right for a sighted user. But NFR-9 mandates announcements only for Presence and permission changes, so the Board mutates under an AT user's virtual cursor with no notification and no stated policy. At 5,000 Tasks and 50 sessions it mutates continuously. Announcing it recreates the storm; not announcing it means the buffer the user is reading is silently stale. *Fix:* decide explicitly — the defensible answer is no per-Task announcement plus a manual "refresh view" affordance and a debounced summary ("3 Tasks changed") — but the decision has to be in the spine.
- **[high]** The virtualised Board tells the AT user a different total from the one on screen, on purpose, with nothing to reconcile it. Component Patterns → Column count mandates "the **true total** for that Status, never the number currently rendered" — correct, and well argued ("a count that tracks virtualisation is a lie"). But the accessibility tree will report only the rendered rows, so the visible chip says 4,812 and the AT user is navigating a list of 30 with no `aria-setsize` / `aria-posinset` requirement anywhere to bridge them. The design creates the mismatch deliberately and then does not close it. *Fix:* require `aria-setsize` = the true total and `aria-posinset` = the Task's true ordinal on every rendered row. This is also what makes the drag announcement's "position 3 of 12" honest.
- **[medium]** Nothing specifies that the permission notice is *persistently* re-readable. A live-region announcement is ephemeral, and announcements fired during a DOM mutation storm — precisely what FR-34 causes, since the editor is being swapped at that instant — are frequently never spoken at all. `EXPERIENCE.md` does specify a visible banner, so the text exists on screen; what is missing is the AT user's route to it. Fixed by the focus move in the critical above, but worth stating as its own requirement. *Fix:* state that the banner persists until dismissed and remains in the reading order.
- **[low]** An assertive interrupt will flush the queued polite Presence announcements. Harmless in effect (a dropped "3 editing" costs nothing) but worth one line so nobody treats a lost Presence announcement as a bug. *Fix:* note it.
- **Handled well, briefly:** the split itself is right — a remote permission change genuinely invalidates what the user is doing, which is exactly what `assertive` is for, and Presence is ambient, which is exactly what `polite` is for. The Accessibility Floor's observation that this is "the accessibility requirement most likely to be missed, because the trigger is remote" is correct and rare to see written down. And "Live regions carry **no cross-Space information**, ever" correctly treats an announcement as a disclosure surface.

## 4. The absent-not-disabled rule

**Verdict: correct as a steady state, wrong as a transition, and no WCAG problem where the brief expected one.** Absence is the better choice for a Viewer's Board and the spine already carries the compensating mechanism. The genuine gaps are the dynamic case and the missing prose statement of Role limits.

- **[high]** Absence is defensible as a property of a surface and hostile as a *transition*, and the spine applies one rule to both. UJ-6's demotion variant: "The editor is replaced by rendered text and the write affordance becomes *absent*, per Role legibility." The user's focus was **in** that editor. Applying absence here means deleting the focused element while focused, with — by the rule's own design — no residual control left behind to explain why the thing they were typing into vanished. For a sighted user the change is visible in peripheral vision; for an AT user the only carrier is the assertive banner, which is not focus-managed (§3). The steady-state argument for absence does not transfer to the interruption case. *Fix:* carve out the transition explicitly — on an FR-34 demotion the write affordance's *removal* must be narrated, and focus must land on the banner, before the surface settles into its absent steady state.
- **[medium-high]** No surface in the browser ever states, in prose, what the acting Role may not do. Isolation and Refusal assigns the within-Space Role refusal "a permission statement naming the capability" — but that path is reachable only by a **write attempt**, and the interface has removed every write affordance for the Role. So the one surface that explains *why* is unreachable from the browser by exactly the user who would want it; in practice it exists only for the API (FR-16). And the obvious route is closed by design: the Role chip is "Display-only. Never interactive, never a menu, never a link to Space settings." *Fix:* extend the context bar's accessible name from the Role *name* to the Role name plus one clause — "Viewer — read only in this Space". It discloses nothing cross-Space, breaks no isolation rule, does not make the chip interactive, and gives every AT user the explanation on arrival rather than on failure.
- **[medium]** For an AT user, an absent control and a not-yet-reached control are indistinguishable; for a sighted user absence has a *shape*. A Viewer's column head is visibly shorter than a Member's — the gap is legible. An AT user traversing the same column head linearly experiences a Board that simply has no create control, which is exactly the honesty contract the design intends, but it is a different quantum of information from what the sighted user gets. Not a WCAG failure; an equity gap that the Role-in-accessible-name mechanism above closes almost entirely. *Fix:* the same fix — a capability clause in the context bar's accessible name is what makes absence *interpretable* rather than merely true.
- **[medium]** Swapping the editor for rendered text silently drops its label. Component Patterns: the description editor is "**Absent entirely for a Viewer** — not read-only, absent." Absence is the right call — AT users routinely cannot distinguish a read-only textarea from a broken one — but nothing says the rendered replacement carries the editor's accessible name. Losing the "Description" label comes free with the swap and nobody would notice it in review. *Fix:* state that the rendered replacement retains the labelled region and its heading.
- **Not a finding, stated because the brief asked:** there is **no 3.2 Predictable failure** here. 3.2.3 Consistent Navigation and 3.2.4 Consistent Identification concern consistency across a set of pages, and absence is applied *consistently* — the same Role always yields the same surface, and each page state is internally coherent. And there is **no 3.3 Input Assistance hook at all**: 3.3.1 and 3.3.3 govern *input errors* ("information provided by the user that is not accepted"), and an absent control generates no input and no error; 3.3.2 governs content that requires input. The absent-not-disabled rule is also the *safer* option against the realistic alternative, since `disabled` removes a control from the tab order and is skipped or announced as a bare "unavailable" by most screen readers, and `aria-disabled` is widely mis-implemented. The isolation requirement that motivates the rule is therefore satisfied at no WCAG cost — the cost is the missing prose explanation, which is cheap to add and does not require reintroducing a disabled state.
- **Handled well, briefly:** the Accessibility Floor already contains the right compensating mechanism — "The **acting Role** is in the accessible name of the context bar, so a screen-reader user establishes standing on arrival without hunting for a chip." That is *better* than a per-control tooltip, because it is stated once on arrival rather than discovered once per failure. The rule is defensible; it needs one clause added, not reversing.

## 5. Focus management

**Verdict: the static cases are specified well and every dynamic case is blank — including one where a specified rule is silently invalid.** Four of the six situations the brief names have no answer in either spine, and two of them can move the wrong Task or lose the user's unsaved text.

- **[critical]** The FR-34 interruption has no focus destination. Covered in §3; restated here because it is a focus defect first and an announcement defect second. Removal inerts the focused node; demotion deletes it. Neither spine says where focus goes, and `assertive` does not move it. *Fix:* focus the banner, and specify the reading order that follows it.
- **[critical]** A recycled virtualised row can put focus on the wrong Task — and then `Space` moves the wrong Task. `EXPERIENCE.md` State Patterns → Board at scale says "Virtualisation is the architecture's call, but **every rendered row keeps its keyboard stop**." That sentence is a tautology (a rendered row is focusable by construction) and it addresses the wrong risk. The real risk is the focused row scrolling out of the virtual window: either its DOM node is destroyed (focus falls to `body`, position lost in a 4,812-row column) or — the common implementation — the node is **recycled for a different Task**, so focus lands silently on the wrong Task and the next `Space` picks *that* one up. The arrow grammar makes this near-certain, since `↓` crosses the window boundary on every screenful. This is a data-corruption path reachable only by keyboard, i.e. only by the users NFR-9 protects. *Fix:* require that keyboard navigation *drives* the virtualiser (moving focus scrolls the window rather than the reverse), that row identity is keyed to the Task id so recycling cannot re-point focus, and that focus is restored by Task id after any window change.
- **[critical]** Nobody owns FR-28 × NFR-9. The PRD says how the Board holds 5,000 Tasks "is the architecture's call; nothing in this document provides it, and the three requirements cannot all hold naively." The architecture spine's 28 ADs never decide it — there is no AD on Board rendering, paging or virtualisation, and §4.8 Board and List Views maps only to AD-13/AD-15/AD-16. `EXPERIENCE.md` then defers back to "the architecture's call". The one requirement the PRD explicitly flags as unsatisfiable naively is unowned across all three spines, and its accessibility half is a release gate. *Fix:* raise this at handoff as an architecture gap, not a UX one. The UX spine's obligation is to state the accessibility contract virtualisation must satisfy — keyboard drives the window, `aria-setsize`/`posinset` carry the true total, focus is restored by id — so that whichever mechanism architecture picks is testable against it.
- **[high]** "Task deleted while open" follows a documented rule that is invalid in that exact case. Component Patterns: "Closing returns focus to the originating Task card." State Patterns → Task deleted while open: "The editor closes to the Board; her unsent text stays visible in a dismissible panel." The originating Task card **has just been deleted**, so the documented return target does not exist and focus falls to `body`. Worse, a dismissible panel holding her only copy of unsent text appears with no focus and no announcement — and it is dismissible, so it can be lost by a stray keypress. An implementer following the stated rule gets nothing. *Fix:* on deletion-while-open, move focus to the retained-text panel, announce it, and specify the fallback return target (the column that held the Task) once it is dismissed.
- **[high]** `inert` — the spine's own word — makes the retained text unreachable, defeating the stated intent. State Patterns: "The editor becomes **inert immediately**… Her unsynchronised text… **stays visible and selectable** so she can copy it." But `inert` is a specified HTML attribute with a precise meaning: content becomes **non-focusable and is removed from the accessibility tree**. Read literally, the spine makes the retained text invisible to AT and unreachable by keyboard — exactly defeating "so she can copy it", and leaving "selectable" true only by pointer. `{components.description-editor-inert}` compounds it by name. *Fix:* say `readonly`, not `inert`. A `readonly` textarea is focusable, selectable, copyable and in the accessibility tree; a `disabled` one is none of those.
- **[high]** The bulk-move in-flight state blocks the region that may hold focus, and the bar is the only cancel route. Covered in §2; the focus-side statement is that "Blocks interaction on the affected columns only" is a focus event with no specified behaviour — if blocking is implemented as `inert` or `aria-hidden` (the natural readings) the focused node is destroyed mid-operation. *Fix:* move focus to the bar on appearance; specify the destination on commit and on cancel.
- **[medium-high]** Space switching replaces the entire surface with no specified focus landing — and it is one of NFR-9's five named flows. Space Context and Role Legibility: switching "changes the entire working surface and carries nothing over". The switcher is a dialog, so the dialog rule returns focus "to the invoking element" — the Space name in the context bar — which is actually the *right* answer, since the acting Role is in that bar's accessible name. But returning focus to an element whose accessible name has changed does not reliably re-announce in all AT, so the user can land in a different Space at a different Role with nothing spoken. *Fix:* state it explicitly — focus the context bar and announce the new Space and Role politely on arrival. Inheriting it from the generic dialog rule is not enough for a gated flow.
- **[medium]** Deleting a Task from the Task detail requires a modal two levels deep, which the spine forbids. Information Architecture: "Modals go **one level deep, never two**." Task detail is a dialog; State Patterns → Delete Task places the confirm on "Task detail". That confirm is level two. Either it replaces the panel's content (and its focus trap and return target need specifying) or it stacks (forbidden). *Fix:* specify that a destructive confirm *replaces* the panel's content in place, with the return target being the panel's own invoker.
- **[medium]** Cold-load skeletons have no AT treatment. State Patterns → Cold load: "Skeletons matching the eventual layout." Without `aria-busy` or being hidden from AT, the user reads a screenful of empty placeholders, then the content silently replaces it with no announcement and no focus anchor. *Fix:* mark skeleton regions `aria-busy="true"` and announce completion politely.
- **[low]** Nothing specifies what happens if the active Space becomes unavailable while the switcher is open (a live FR-34 event mid-switch). Narrow, but this pair is otherwise careful about interruption. *Fix:* one line — the switcher closes to the refusal surface.
- **Handled well, briefly:** the static specifications are good and specific — "Focus trapped, returned to the invoking element on close", "Closing returns focus to the originating Task card", "`Tab` order follows reading order on every surface", and a focus ring that is "Never removed, never replaced with a colour change or a border swap" with a real 2px offset and a dedicated token. Most design spines do not get this far.

## 6. i18n and accessibility interaction

**Verdict: better than the brief's premise, with two real failures the spine has not seen.** Letter-spacing does not harm braille and CSS uppercase does not spell out — both concerns in the brief are unfounded and I would rather say so than manufacture findings. The genuine problems are 1.4.12, locale-unsafe uppercasing, and the RTL arrow keys.

- **[high]** WCAG 1.4.12 Text Spacing (AA) is unmentioned and the type scale is built to fail it. 1.4.12 requires no loss of content or functionality when the user overrides **line-height to 1.5×**, letter-spacing to 0.12×, word-spacing to 0.16× and paragraph spacing to 2×. `{typography.role-label}` sets `lineHeight: '1'` — a line box exactly the glyph height, which cannot absorb any growth: at 1.5× a 9px chip needs a 13.5px line box inside a `{rounded.sm}` chip designed for 9px. `{typography.column-head}` is 1.2 and `{typography.meta}` is 1.4, all inside a layout on a **3px** unit with `card-pad-y: 9px`. A user stylesheet applying 1.4.12's values to this design clips or overlaps. *Fix:* raise every line-height to ≥1.5 or specify that all chip and card boxes size to content with no fixed heights; then test the pair against 1.4.12's four overrides as a gate item.
- **[high]** RTL mirrors the drag direction and says nothing about the arrow keys — so the two spines specify opposite behaviours. Internationalisation: "The Board's column order and the **drag direction mirror** under RTL". Interaction Primitives: "`← →` — between columns" and "`← →` move it between columns". Under RTL, does `→` move to the next column in *reading* order (visually left) or to the visually-right column? Both are defensible, they are opposite, and the mirroring mandate covers the gesture but not the keys. Getting it wrong means an RTL keyboard user moves Tasks the opposite way from what they see — and this sits exactly on the seam between the two sections. *Fix:* bind the keys to *logical* direction (`→` = inline-end = next column in reading order), consistent with the logical-properties rule the same section already mandates, and say so.
- **[high]** The 10px monospace metadata is the mandated non-colour carrier of Presence — and therefore the smallest text in the product is the one NFR-9's compliance argument rests on. `{components.presence-indicator}` uses `{typography.meta}`, 10px mono, and its text count exists *specifically* to satisfy "not conveyed by colour or position alone". Compounding it, `DESIGN.md`'s own Typography §2 notes that `system-mono` "will fall back unpredictably for CJK and Indic text, and the fallback is often not monospaced at all" — so the rendered size of that mandated string is platform-dependent, and monospace stacks commonly have a smaller x-height-to-em ratio than the sans stack, making 10px mono read visually smaller than 10px sans. There is no numeric SC on font size, so this is not a named failure; it is a bad place to rest the argument. *Fix:* raise `{typography.meta}` to 11px and set the Presence count specifically in `system-sans`, since it is prose ("2 editing") rather than a system fact — the monospace rationale genuinely does not apply to it.
- **[medium]** Uppercase must be stated as presentational, or the product's most important accessible name becomes a spelled-out string. The failure mode the brief asks about is real but narrow: CSS `text-transform: uppercase` leaves the DOM text unchanged, so AT reads the original casing and nothing is spelled out. The exposure is when uppercase is baked into the **string** — and Yello has exactly that risk in the worst possible place: the Role chip reads "Owner / Admin / Member / Viewer verbatim" and is styled uppercase, *and* the acting Role is placed "in the accessible name of the context bar". If a developer or translator writes "VIEWER", the accessible name of the single most important accessibility affordance in the product becomes "V-I-E-W-E-R" in JAWS and VoiceOver. Neither spine says the casing is presentational. *Fix:* state that all uppercase is `text-transform` and that copy resources hold sentence case — a one-line rule that also protects the externalised-copy requirement.
- **[medium]** The per-script fallback covers case-less scripts and misses the case-*ful* ones where uppercasing is lossy. `DESIGN.md` Typography §1 handles Arabic, Hebrew, CJK, Thai and Indic correctly and thoughtfully. But `text-transform: uppercase` is locale-sensitive and wrong in several cased scripts: Turkish dotless ı → I changes the word, German ß → SS changes length, Greek strips accents and alters final sigma. A Status named in Turkish can be uppercased into a different word. *Fix:* extend the existing per-script rule to require `text-transform` under a locale-aware `lang` attribute, and exclude Turkish, Azeri and Greek from the treatment as the rule already excludes the case-less scripts.
- **[medium]** `{typography.role-label}` at 9px is in direct tension with the requirement it serves. UJ-4 requires the Role "legible at all times", and Colors calls the Role chip "permanent chrome, not a badge". It is specified at **9px** mono, 700, 0.07em. The spine asserts the requirement and specifies the size that undermines it. *Fix:* 10–11px minimum for the Role chip; it is the one piece of metadata the product cannot afford to have squinted at.
- **Not findings, stated because the brief asked:** CSS `letter-spacing` inserts no characters into the DOM, so **braille output and screen-reader pronunciation are unaffected** by the 0.12em tracking — a braille display renders the text node, not the rendering. And monospace-for-metadata carries **no cognitive-accessibility SC** at AA (1.4.8 Visual Presentation is AAA and out of scope); it is a legitimate design choice and the "system fact rather than decorative badge" rationale is sound. The problems are the *size* it is set at and the *fallback unpredictability*, both above — not the typeface class.
- **Handled well, briefly:** the per-script uppercase fallback and the "metadata must never be aligned by character count" rule are both genuinely good, both correctly identified as behavioural obligations rather than visual ones, and both the kind of thing that is normally discovered during localisation rather than specified before it. The logical-properties mandate and the server-clock rule for relative time are also right.

## 7. Text scaling and reflow

**Verdict: the reflow *architecture* is right and the density arithmetic defeats it.** The `<768px` one-column pager is the correct answer to 1.4.10 and deserves credit. But every dimension in the design is an absolute px, the pager itself is unspecified as a control, and the spine promises a 44px touch target that its own component values contradict.

- **[high]** Every font size and every spacing value is absolute px, so the design cannot honour a user's browser text-size preference at all. `{typography}` is 9–16px and `{spacing}` is a 3px unit, all absolute. A user who has raised their default font size — the most common low-vision accommodation and the mechanism 1.4.4 is really about — sees **no change**. Full-page zoom still works, so 1.4.4 is arguably met by the zoom route, but "we pass because Ctrl+= works" is not a position I would want to defend at a release gate on a product whose smallest text is 9px. *Fix:* move the type scale to `rem` (10px meta → 0.625rem), keep px only for hairlines and radii, and size card padding in `em` so it tracks the text.
- **[high]** Text-only zoom to 200% clips, because the px spacing does not grow with the glyphs. At 200%, `{typography.task-title}`'s line box goes from 17.55px to **35.1px** inside a card whose `card-pad-y` stays at 9px and whose `card-stack-gap` stays at 6px. `{typography.role-label}` at `lineHeight: '1'` is the guaranteed first casualty. 1.4.4 requires no loss of content or functionality. *Fix:* the same rem/em conversion; then verify at 200% text-only, not just page zoom.
- **[high]** At 320px the Task card's metadata row has 282px and an unbounded number of things to put in it, with no stated overflow rule. 320px minus column padding (9px × 2) minus `card-pad-x` (10px × 2) leaves **282px**. Into that the design puts Label chips (Labels are user-defined per Space, and nothing in FR-22 or either spine bounds how many a Task may carry), plus the Presence indicator ("2 editing" plus a 6px dot), plus a right-pushed 20px Assignee avatar. No wrapping, truncation or overflow behaviour is specified — and `DESIGN.md`'s own Motion section already warns the row is fragile ("a moving avatar in the metadata row shifts the Task title"). Unbounded chips in a fixed 282px row is horizontal overflow inside the card, which is a 1.4.10 failure in the exact layout the audit will be conducted in. *Fix:* specify the row's overflow — chips wrap to a second line, or truncate to N with a "+3" affordance — and bound Labels per Task.
- **[high]** The Status pager is the sole navigation route in the reflowed layout and is completely unspecified. Responsive & Platform, `<768px`: "**One column at a time**, with a Status pager." That is the only way to reach a Status below 768px — and 320px CSS width is precisely the 1.4.10 test condition (1280px at 400% zoom), so the pager *is* the surface under audit. Its keyboard behaviour, accessible name, whether it is a tablist or a listbox, how it announces the column change, and where focus lands on page are all absent. *Fix:* specify it as a tablist over the effective Status set, with the column as its panel, arrow-key navigation, and a polite announcement of the new Status and its true count on change.
- **[high]** "Touch targets ≥ 44px" is asserted in one spine and contradicted by the other for every named button. Accessibility Floor states the 44px floor. `DESIGN.md` then specifies `{components.button-primary}` with `{spacing.2}` 13px padding over a 14px/1.55 body line box = **33.7px** tall; `{components.avatar}` at **20px**; `{components.presence-indicator}` `dot-size` at **6px**; `{components.role-chip}` at 9px type; `{components.column-count}` at 10px type. The avatar and the Role chip are explicitly non-interactive (correct, so the floor does not apply), but the switcher chevron, the column create affordance, the pager, and every dialog button are interactive and none is specified at 44px. To be precise about the gate: WCAG 2.1 AA has **no** target-size criterion (2.5.5 is AAA at 44×44; 2.2's 2.5.8 is 24×24 AA, which 33.7px clears), so this is a **self-consistency failure rather than a 2.1 AA failure** — but a release gate audited against the spine's own stated floor will fail it, and the density rationale means nobody notices until an auditor measures. *Fix:* either raise interactive components to 44px minimum (padding only — glyph sizes can stay) or restate the floor as 24px to match WCAG 2.2 AA and say so deliberately.
- **[medium]** The density premise does not survive the zoom level the gate is tested at, and the spine should say so. Layout & Spacing justifies the density as a requirement: "This design fits roughly twice the Tasks of a comfortable one. That is the point," because "every Task that fits on screen is one fewer row to virtualise and one fewer keyboard stop to manage." At 200% text a 6-word title wraps from 1–2 lines to 4–5, and the density advantage — and with it the stated FR-28 mitigation — evaporates. That is not a failure, but it means the virtualisation load the design claims to reduce returns in full at exactly the accessibility setting the gate exercises. *Fix:* record it as an accepted cost, the way `.memlog.md` records the border decision, so the FR-28 argument is not read as holding at all zoom levels.
- **[low]** `[ASSUMPTION: no user-facing comfortable/compact control in v1]` is defensible on state-matrix grounds but removes the cheapest accommodation available. A density toggle is a common low-vision and cognitive-load accommodation, and this is the densest plausible default. *Fix:* leave the assumption, but note the accessibility angle in it so a later reader sees the full cost.
- **Handled well, briefly:** the `<768px` one-column-with-pager approach is the **right** answer to 1.4.10 and should be credited rather than defended. 1.4.10 exempts "content which requires two-dimensional layout for usage or meaning", and a Kanban board is exactly the case people claim that exemption for — this spine declines the exemption and eliminates the horizontal axis instead, which is the stronger position. And because 400% zoom on a 1280px monitor yields a 320px CSS viewport, the `<768px` rule fires automatically at the test condition: the compliance works by construction rather than by luck. The Task card also genuinely clears 44px on its own arithmetic (9 + 9 padding + 17.55 title + 14 metadata = **49.55px**), so "the long-press lift target is the whole Task card" is a correct and load-bearing decision.
