Three lenses ran in parallel against the first complete draft: the rubric walker, an accessibility audit, and an adversarial isolation audit. All three independently reached the same shape of conclusion — the pair is unusually disciplined for a first draft, and the places it fails are places where a stated principle did not survive arithmetic or implementation reality. Flow coverage and shape fit were strong on the rubric; the isolation audit found no specified leak and cleared 25 named surfaces; and the accessibility audit credited the focus-ring separation, Presence-as-dot-plus-text, the per-script uppercase fallback and the explicit refusal to let the long-press gesture discharge NFR-9 as decisions most design specs get wrong.

The accessibility verdict was nonetheless not gate-ready, and it was right. Three failures were real and structural: no single-pointer alternative to the Board drag on a touch tablet at 768–1279px — a WCAG 2.5.1 Level A failure, not AA; a keyboard grammar bound to arrow keys that NVDA and JAWS consume in browse mode, so the accessible path was unreachable by exactly the population NFR-9 protects; and an FR-34 interruption that makes the focused node readonly or replaces it outright with no specified focus destination, on the requirement the PRD names as the criterion the product should be judged on. A single fix — an explicit non-gesture Move control at every breakpoint — closed the first two.

The isolation audit's contribution was to show that the spines specified the server-facing half of isolation and were nearly silent on the client-facing half. AD-2 enforces scoping in the database; AD-11 puts a full replica in the client; SM-1's suite exercises requests. A leak rendered from client memory issues no request, so the release gate structurally cannot see it — and the replica's lifetime across a Space switch, a sign-out and a lease invalidation was never specified. Alongside it: 409, never 404 stated without AD-26's Membership qualifier turns the offer endpoint into a cross-Space existence oracle, and the email-address rule from §6.1 appeared nowhere in either spine.

On the author's own contrast table: eight of twelve figures were arithmetically wrong. None flipped a pass into a fail — nine were understated — but the table was presented as verification against a release gate, and its one "little headroom" warning sat on the token with 10% clearance while the actual thinnest margin, at 2%, went unremarked. Two genuine AA failures were hiding in pairs the table never stated: the accent text link against body copy (2.66:1, below 1.4.1) and the Role chip fill against its own ground (1.05:1, invisible as a container). Both are now fixed, the table is recomputed, and the light theme is stated in full.

106 of 111 findings are resolved. Two are escalated to bmad-architecture because the fix is not in the interface. Three remain open by deliberate decision and are marked as such.

2 · Token completeness

adequate

220 frontmatter token paths and every {path.to.token} reference extracted across both files — 67 distinct in DESIGN.md, 10 in EXPERIENCE.md. All resolve. All 14 colour roles carry a hex value and a light/dark pair, so there were no critical colour gaps. The failures were in the verification layer rather than the token layer.

highfixed

Eight of twelve contrast figures were wrong, in a table presented as verified against a release gate

DESIGN.md § Colors
Both reviewers recomputed independently and agreed. Nine figures understated, three overstated. No error flipped a pass into a fail — but no alternate luminance formula reproduces the pattern, so they had been estimated rather than computed. Worse than the arithmetic: the one "passes, with little headroom — do not darken" warning sat on accent at 4.96 (10% clear) while the genuine thinnest margin, border-hairline-light on surface-column-light at 3.06 (2% clear), was described as "clear".
Fix: all figures recomputed from hex with the WCAG 2.x formula, the formula stated, both themes tabulated in full, and the light border raised to #6B7794 for real margin. The warning now names the token that actually has none.
highfixed

Accent text links fail WCAG 1.4.1 as specified

DESIGN.md § Components
accent against text-primary is 2.66:1 dark and 2.55:1 light, below the 3:1 required when colour alone distinguishes a link from surrounding body text — and no non-colour cue was mandated anywhere. The accent passes handsomely against the background and fails against the text beside it, which is the pair that matters for a link.
Fix: underline mandatory on inline links; new text-link component; both stated in Do's and Don'ts.
highfixed

Every border pair drops below 3:1 under partial pixel coverage, and one component guarantees it

DESIGN.md § Colors
A 1px border antialiases whenever its edge lands off a device-pixel boundary — which a 3px spacing grid does at the 1.25×/1.5×/1.75× display scales common on Windows and Android. At 80% coverage every pair fails. And task-card-lifted's rotate(-1deg) antialiases its border along its entire length unconditionally, so the one object whose boundary matters most — the Task in your hand — was the one guaranteed to render under the gate.
Fix: new borders token group; structural hairline raised to 1.5px with device-pixel snapping; 2px emphasis border on the lifted card.
highfixed

The Label colour constraint was stated against one theme, and the same user-chosen colour renders in both

DESIGN.md § Components
The constraint required 3:1 against the dark card ground only. The two card grounds are 17 stops apart, so a fill tuned to near-black routinely fails against white — a whole class of user-generatable 1.4.11 failures in a release-gated product. The ΔE 10 rule also named no colour space (ΔE76 and ΔE2000 differ by roughly a factor of two at these chromas) and omitted accent and presence.
Fix: 3:1 against both card grounds simultaneously; ΔE2000 named; accent and presence added to the exclusion set.
highfixed

The light theme had hex values but no component-level resolution mechanism

DESIGN.md frontmatter
Every components.* entry referenced dark tokens, and 11 of the 14 -light tokens were referenced nowhere. A consumer extracting the component map got a dark-only system whose light counterpart existed as a prose naming convention.
Fix: the suffix pair is now stated as a theme-selection mechanism resolved once at the theme boundary, with components consuming semantic names only — and reaching for a -light token directly declared a defect.
mediumfixed

The focus-ring rationale was arithmetically hollow

DESIGN.md § Colors
The document justified separating focus-ring from accent on the grounds that it "would vanish against accented controls" — and focus-ring against accent is 1.45:1, so it would. What actually protects it is outline-offset: 2px putting the ring on the ground behind. The Do/Don't table was guarding the wrong property.
Fix: both tokens kept, rationale corrected to geometry, and the real rule stated — never inset the ring, never reduce the offset to 0.
mediumfixed

Accent and danger are indistinguishable by luminance while the document made the border the sole signal

DESIGN.md § Components
accent vs danger is 1.19:1 dark and 1.08:1 light — they differ by hue alone, so under deuteranopia the accent-bordered bulk-move bar and the danger-bordered destructive confirm converge. Yet the spec said "The border is the signal; there is no icon and no illustration." Compliance survived only because EXPERIENCE.md supplies the copy.
Fix: the copy is now named as the signal and the border as reinforcement, in both places.
mediumfixed

The danger token was used against the document's own rule for it

DESIGN.md § Colors, Do's and Don'ts
Do's and Don'ts reserved danger for "the genuinely irreversible" and forbade it on "ordinary refusals" — while the FR-34 revoked-editor component used a danger border. Losing access is a refusal. An implementer following the table and one following the component would build different things.
Fix: new revoked-edge token, distinct in name even where it resolves to the same value, so the component and the rule stop contradicting each other.
mediumfixed

Absolute px type ignores a user's font-size preference; line-height 1 cannot absorb a 1.4.12 override

DESIGN.md § Typography
Every size was absolute px, including role-label at 9px carrying the permanent Role indicator. Page zoom scales px so 1.4.4 is arguably met by that route, but text-only resize is not — and lineHeight: '1' is a line box exactly the glyph height, which clips the moment a user applies 1.4.12's 1.5× line-height override.
Fix: whole scale moved to rem; every line-height ≥ 1.5; role-label and meta raised to 0.6875rem; px retained only on hairlines, radii and offsets.
mediumfixed

Six components shipped with no foreground token

DESIGN.md frontmatter
In a document whose stated method is that load-bearing combinations are "stated rather than assumed", bulk-move-bar, column-count, label-chip, picker, space-switcher and avatar specified background, border and typography and omitted text colour — each an unverified pair as written.
Fix: explicit foreground added to each.
mediumfixed

Off-scale pixel literals, two of them bypassing a token defined as exactly that value

DESIGN.md frontmatter
10px appeared as a bare literal in context-bar.padding and space-switcher.row-padding while spacing.card-pad-x was defined as 10px.
Fix: component-internal padding moved to named rem tokens (card-pad-*, control-pad-*) and referenced rather than restated — which also makes padding grow with text under zoom.
lowfixed

Role chip fill invisible against its own ground in both themes

DESIGN.md § Components
role-chip on surface-card is 1.05:1 dark, 1.29:1 light, with no border. Not a WCAG failure — the chip is non-interactive and its text is fine at 8.56:1 — but "monospace, uppercase, 2px corners" described a chip nobody could see, and UJ-4's central bet is that the Role reads as a distinct permanent system fact.
Fix: 1px border-hairline border, which reads at 3.44:1 against the chip fill.

highfixed

Revocation-while-disconnected unhandled, and the two rows that would compose to cover it contradicted each other in committed copy

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
"Disconnected. Your changes are held locally." promises application. Access-ending requires that unsynchronised text is not applied — and its mechanism is a push (AD-9) that by definition cannot reach a disconnected client. So the User is told their changes are held, is removed while offline, reconnects, and the product must retract a promise it made in writing. This is the composition of FR-33 and FR-34 — the seam PRD §4.9 calls "the requirement most likely to be got wrong".
Fix: a dedicated state added that resolves to "Access ended." rather than to a reconciliation failure (the text was never admissible, not lost to a fault), with an explicit ban on showing a sync-succeeded state first. Copy softened to "not yet sent".

Accessibility review

not gate-readyreview-accessibility.md

6 critical, 29 high, 17 medium, 4 low. The reviewer credited the pair as "unusually accessibility-literate for a design document" and named five decisions most specs get wrong and this one got right on purpose. It then found that the compliance argument rested in three places on statements that do not survive arithmetic or implementation — and that one of the three is a Level A failure rather than AA. All six criticals are resolved.

criticalfixed

No single-pointer alternative to the Board drag at 768–1279px — WCAG 2.5.1, Level A

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
2.5.1 requires all functionality using a path-based gesture to be operable with a single pointer without a path. The touch user got only "long-press to lift, then drag". The non-gesture alternative existed only below 768px — so a touch tablet in that band had no non-path route to a cross-column move, and the keyboard path is no alternative for a device with no keyboard. Inside the Board, one of NFR-9's five named flows.
Fix: an explicit Move control made the canonical path at every breakpoint, with the gestures reframed as accelerators. FR-29 already defines Status-change-as-move, so only the affordance was missing. Also pre-satisfies WCAG 2.2's 2.5.7.
criticalfixed

The entire keyboard grammar is unreachable in screen-reader browse mode

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
In NVDA's and JAWS's default browse mode, arrow keys move the virtual cursor and the application never receives them. So a screen-reader user could not pick up a Task, move it, or cancel — and neither spine mentioned that the mode exists. NFR-9's "every Board operation available by pointer is available by keyboard" was met for a sighted keyboard user and not for the population the requirement is written for.
Fix: the same Move control, which works in browse mode with no mode switching. role="application" deliberately rejected as a known trap. The arrow grammar is retained as an accelerator with its application-mode requirement stated.
criticalfixed

assertive announces the FR-34 interruption and leaves the user stranded

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
aria-live="assertive" interrupts the current utterance; it does not move focus. The FR-34 event makes the focused element inert or replaces it outright — and replacing a focused node resets focus to document.body in every browser. The user hears "Access ended", then finds themselves nowhere, in a surface whose write affordances have all just become absent, with the retained text having no place in the reading order. On the requirement the PRD says to judge the product on.
Fix: the banner is focusable (role="alert", tabindex="-1"), focus moves to it, it persists until dismissed, and the retained text is the next stop in the reading order.
criticalfixed

A recycled virtualised row can put focus on the wrong Task — and then Space moves the wrong Task

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
"Every rendered row keeps its keyboard stop" is a tautology and addresses the wrong risk. The real risk is the focused row scrolling out of the virtual window: either its node is destroyed (focus falls to body, position lost in a 4,812-row column) or — the common implementation — the node is recycled for a different Task. The arrow grammar makes this near-certain, since ↓ crosses the window boundary every screenful. A data-corruption path reachable only by keyboard.
Fix: keyboard navigation drives the virtualiser rather than the reverse; row identity keyed to the Task id so recycling cannot re-point focus; focus restored by Task id after any window change.
criticalfixed

No focus destination for any remote event

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
Four of six dynamic situations had no answer in either spine. Two could lose the user's unsaved text. One documented rule was silently invalid: "closing returns focus to the originating Task card" cannot apply when the Task has just been deleted.
Fix: a focus-destination table covering seven remote and dynamic events, including the deleted-Task case and the recycled-row case.
criticalescalated

Nobody owns FR-28 × NFR-5 × NFR-9

all three spines
The PRD says how the Board holds 5,000 Tasks "is the architecture's call; nothing in this document provides it, and the three requirements cannot all hold naively." The architecture's 28 ADs never decide it — no AD covers Board rendering, paging or virtualisation, and §4.8 maps only to AD-13/15/16. EXPERIENCE.md deferred back. A three-way deferral on a release-gated requirement.
Fix: escalated to bmad-architecture. The UX obligation — stating the accessibility contract any mechanism must satisfy — is now discharged: keyboard drives the window, aria-setsize/aria-posinset carry the true total, focus restores by id. Whoever picks the mechanism must decide focus and announcement behaviour in the same pass.
highfixed

Space globally rebound without scope, colliding with native activation and page scroll

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
Space is the native activation key for every button and the native page-down for a scroll container — and Board columns are scroll containers by the spine's own rule. On a focused column-head control, no scope told the implementer which meaning applied.
Fix: the pick-up binding applies only when a Task card holds focus; Space keeps its native meaning everywhere else.
highfixed

Three unspecified keyboard behaviours that no test could be written against

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
"Preserving position where possible" admits at least three divergent readings. The keyboard drop had no destination position at all — and AD-15 computes a fractional index between two concrete neighbours, so the implementation needed a neighbour pair the spec never supplied. And the live-region announcement, which for a blind user replaces the entire visual drop-zone system, was one unspecified sentence.
Fix: sticky-origin-index with clamping; destination is the same ordinal clamped to column length; announcement string shape specified with destination, ordinal and total.
highfixed

The long-press scroll-intent rule had no slop radius and covered one axis

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
Read literally, 1px of finger jitter cancelled every lift. And the rule was single-axis while the spine's own 768–1279px breakpoint creates a horizontal Board scroll, where a pan is indistinguishable from the start of a cross-column drag.
Fix: a 10px slop token, and the rule made axis-agnostic.
highfixed

Presence announcement storms guaranteed at every NFR-8 bound

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
10 concurrent editors cycling produces up to 20 transitions in seconds, and polite queues rather than coalescing — so the backlog outlives the events and talks over the user's own typing echo. Worse, Presence was placed on Task cards too, so Board-level churn scaling with 50 concurrent Sessions would route into the same region.
Fix: settled-count announcements debounced to ~5s, scoped to the open Task only, suppressed while typing; card-level Presence renders visually without a live region; permission changes never throttled.
highfixed

The virtualised Board tells assistive technology a different total from the one on screen

EXPERIENCE.md § Component Patterns
The column count is mandated as the true total — correct, and well argued. But the accessibility tree reports only rendered rows, so the chip says 4,812 while the user navigates a list of 30, with nothing bridging them. The design created the mismatch deliberately and did not close it.
Fix: aria-setsize and aria-posinset required on every rendered row — which is also what makes the drag announcement's "position 3 of 12" honest.
highfixed

inert — the spine's own word — made the retained text unreachable, defeating its stated purpose

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
inert is a specified HTML attribute: content becomes non-focusable and is removed from the accessibility tree. Read literally, the spine made the retained text invisible to AT and unreachable by keyboard — exactly defeating "so she can copy it" — and left "selectable" true only by pointer.
Fix: readonly throughout, in both spines and in the component token name, with a note explaining why inert and disabled are both wrong.
highfixed

Absence is defensible as a steady state and hostile as a transition

EXPERIENCE.md § Space Context
On an FR-34 demotion the spine deleted the focused editor with — by the rule's own design — no residual control left to explain why the thing being typed into vanished. The steady-state argument for absence does not transfer to the interruption case. Notably the reviewer confirmed there is no 3.2 or 3.3 failure in the rule itself, and that absence is the safer choice against disabled.
Fix: the transition carved out — removal is narrated first, focus lands on the banner, and only then does the surface settle into its absent steady state.
highfixed

No surface ever states in prose what the acting Role may not do

EXPERIENCE.md § Space Context
The within-Space Role refusal is reachable only by a write attempt — and the interface has removed every write affordance for that Role. So the one surface that explains why was unreachable from the browser by exactly the user who would want it, existing in practice only for the API. The obvious route was closed by design, since the Role chip is deliberately non-interactive.
Fix: the context bar's accessible name extended from the Role name to the Role plus a capability clause — "Viewer, read only in this Space". Discloses nothing cross-Space, keeps the chip non-interactive, and explains on arrival rather than on failure.
highfixed

WCAG 1.4.12 Text Spacing unmentioned, and the type scale built to fail it

DESIGN.md § Typography
role-label set lineHeight: '1' — a line box exactly the glyph height, which cannot absorb any growth. At 1.4.12's mandated 1.5× override a 9px chip needs a 13.5px line box inside a chip designed for 9px, on a layout with a 3px unit.
Fix: every line-height ≥ 1.5; chips and cards size to content with no fixed heights; the four 1.4.12 overrides named as a gate item.
highfixed

RTL mirrors the drag direction and says nothing about the arrow keys — so the two spines specify opposite behaviours

EXPERIENCE.md §§ Internationalisation, Interaction Primitives
The mirroring mandate covered the gesture but not the keys. Under RTL, does → move to the next column in reading order or the visually-right one? Both defensible, opposite, and an RTL keyboard user would move Tasks the opposite way from what they see.
Fix: keys bound to logical direction — → is inline-end — consistent with the logical-properties rule the same section already mandates.
highfixed

The mandated non-colour carrier of Presence was the smallest text in the product

DESIGN.md § Typography
The Presence count exists specifically to satisfy "not conveyed by colour or position alone" — and was set at 10px mono, whose non-Latin fallback the document itself notes is unpredictable and often not monospaced, with a smaller effective x-height than the sans stack.
Fix: a dedicated presence-count token at 0.6875rem in system-sans. It is prose, not a system fact, so the monospace rationale genuinely does not apply.
highfixed

Absolute px type and spacing, so a raised default font size changes nothing

DESIGN.md §§ Typography, Layout
Full-page zoom still works, so 1.4.4 is arguably met by that route — but "we pass because Ctrl+= works" is a poor position at a release gate on a product whose smallest text was 9px. At 200% text-only the task-title line box doubles inside padding that does not grow.
Fix: type in rem, component padding in rem so it tracks the text, px only on hairlines and radii; verification at 200% text-only named as a gate item.
highfixed

The Status pager is the sole navigation route in the reflowed layout and was completely unspecified

EXPERIENCE.md § Responsive & Platform
400% zoom on a 1280px monitor yields a 320px CSS viewport, so the pager is the surface a 1.4.10 audit is conducted on — and its keyboard behaviour, accessible name, widget role, announcement and initial focus were all absent.
Fix: specified as a tablist over the effective Status set with arrow-key navigation and a polite announcement of the new Status and its true count.
highfixed

"Touch targets ≥ 44px" asserted in one spine and contradicted by the other for every named button

both spines
A self-consistency failure rather than a 2.1 AA failure — WCAG 2.1 AA has no target-size criterion at all, and 2.5.5's 44×44 is AAA. But a gate audited against the spine's own stated floor would fail it, and the density rationale meant nobody would notice until an auditor measured.
Fix: the floor restated as {spacing.target-min} 24px per WCAG 2.2 AA's 2.5.8 — the real current AA criterion — applied as a minimum height on every interactive component, with the reasoning stated. The Task card clears 49px on its own arithmetic anyway.
highfixed

The bulk move had no keyboard or focus story, and the only cancel route was a transient bar

EXPERIENCE.md §§ Component Patterns, State Patterns
A bar appears somewhere on the Board and simultaneously the region that may hold focus is blocked. A keyboard user initiating a 4,812-Task move could lose the only route to cancelling it — and if blocking were implemented as inert or aria-hidden, the focused node is destroyed mid-operation.
Fix: focus moves to the bar on appearance, it carries role="status", and destinations are specified for both commit and cancel.
mediumfixed

Uppercase not stated as presentational, in the worst possible place

DESIGN.md § Typography
CSS text-transform leaves the DOM text alone, so nothing is spelled out — but if uppercase is baked into the string, it is. And Yello has exactly that risk on the Role chip, whose value is also placed in the accessible name of the context bar: a resource written VIEWER becomes "V-I-E-W-E-R" in JAWS and VoiceOver, degrading the single most important accessibility affordance in the product.
Fix: all uppercase is text-transform; copy resources hold sentence case; stated in both spines.
mediumfixed

The per-script fallback covered case-less scripts and missed the case-ful ones where uppercasing is lossy

DESIGN.md § Typography
Arabic, Hebrew, CJK, Thai and Indic were handled correctly. But text-transform: uppercase is locale-sensitive and wrong in several cased scripts: Turkish dotless ı → I changes the word, German ß → SS changes length, Greek strips accents and alters final sigma. A Status named in Turkish can be uppercased into a different word.
Fix: the treatment applies under a locale-aware lang attribute, with Turkish, Azeri and Greek excluded alongside the case-less scripts.
mediumfixed

Esc carried three meanings and two collided on one surface

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
The description editor lives inside the Task detail panel, which is a dialog. Pressing Esc in the editor — leave the editor, or close the panel? For an AT user that is the difference between exiting a textarea and losing the panel and its unsaved text.
Fix: precedence stated — innermost meaning wins.
mediumfixed

Deleting a Task from Task detail required a modal two levels deep, which the spine forbids

EXPERIENCE.md §§ IA, State Patterns
Task detail is a dialog; the delete confirm was placed on Task detail. That confirm is level two.
Fix: destructive confirms replace their invoking panel's content in place, stated in both IA and Component Patterns.
mediumfixed

Whether Board mutations are announced at all was undecided, and both answers need stating

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
Remote edits push Tasks between columns, and DESIGN.md deliberately makes this silent visually — right for a sighted user. But NFR-9 mandates announcements only for Presence and permission changes, so the Board mutates under an AT user's virtual cursor with no policy. Announcing recreates the storm; not announcing leaves the buffer silently stale.
Fix: decided — no per-Task announcement, a debounced summary, and a manual refresh affordance.
mediumfixed

Cold-load skeletons had no AT treatment; the description editor's label was dropped on swap

EXPERIENCE.md §§ State Patterns, Component Patterns
Without aria-busy the user reads a screenful of placeholders then the content silently replaces it. And swapping the editor for rendered text loses the "Description" label for free, which nobody would notice in review.
Fix: skeletons marked aria-busy="true" with completion announced; the rendered replacement retains the labelled region and heading.
mediumfixed

Space switching replaces the entire surface with no specified focus landing — on a gated flow

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
The generic dialog rule returns focus to the invoking element, which is actually the right target. But returning focus to an element whose accessible name has changed does not reliably re-announce, so a user can land in a different Space at a different Role with nothing spoken.
Fix: stated explicitly — focus the context bar, announce the new Space and Role politely on arrival.
lowopen

The density premise does not survive the zoom level the gate is tested at

DESIGN.md § Layout & Spacing
Density is justified as an FR-28 mitigation — twice the Tasks on screen, so fewer rows to virtualise. At 200% text a six-word title wraps from two lines to five, and the advantage evaporates at exactly the accessibility setting the gate exercises.
Fix: open by decision. Not a failure, so nothing is changed — but recorded in DESIGN.md as an accepted cost, so the FR-28 argument is not read as holding at every zoom level. The related note that a density toggle is itself a low-vision accommodation is now in the assumption.

Isolation review

no specified leakreview-isolation.md

2 critical, 7 high, 4 medium, 6 low — and every one in the "under-specified so an implementer could easily create a leak" category rather than "specified leak". The audit cleared 25 named surfaces, called the Space switcher "the strongest part of the review" (identity-only, stated four times across both spines with no contradiction, and the badge attack pre-empted by name), and credited "live regions carry no cross-Space information, ever" as the correct rule, rarely written down. Its central point: the spines specified the server-facing half of isolation and were almost silent on the client-facing half.

criticalfixed

The local replica's lifetime is never specified, so Space content survives every boundary event by default

EXPERIENCE.md § Foundation
The mechanism is the default behaviour of every implementation of AD-11, not an exotic mistake. A Blazor WASM client keeps a replica in memory across a client-side route change so switching back is instant; FR-33's five-minute reconnect window and the "held locally" state push it further toward durable browser storage. Then: switch to Space B while a live replica of A remains resident; Membership in A is removed and the lease invalidated, with nothing telling the client to drop it; switch back and the cached Board renders optimistically, before the 404 lands. AD-7 forbids only credentials in localStorage, so A's Task text can survive sign-out on a shared machine. SM-1's suite runs request-level cases, so a purely client-side render would not fail it — which is what makes this the most likely route to a verified disclosure in a shipped build.
Fix: a named, testable lifecycle rule — Space switch, sign-out, Account switch, lease invalidation and back/forward each purge every replica, projection, cached Board, queued frame and pending announcement for the departed Space, synchronously before any render; no Space content in localStorage/sessionStorage/IndexedDB; optimistic rendering never precedes authorisation on first read.
criticalescalated

The two 404 cases cannot have equal latency as implemented — a timing oracle

EXPERIENCE.md § Isolation and Refusal
The spine asserted "same latency". AD-20 requires an AccessRefusal row for every Space-boundary 404 and not for an ordinary not-found, so the boundary case performs a database INSERT the other does not. Measurable, repeatable, and available to anyone holding a Membership in one Space: probe an id, time the refusal, learn whether it exists somewhere in Yello. AD-3 — unlike AD-23 — never makes duration part of its contract, so nothing upstream catches it, and asserting the property in a UX document reads to an implementer as satisfied-by-construction.
Fix: escalated to bmad-architecture. The claim is softened to "every respect the interface controls" with the gap named. The architectural options are to write the refusal record off the response path, write an equivalent record for the ordinary case, or pad boundary refusals to a fixed floor — and SM-1 needs a timing case, since it currently measures disclosure rather than duration.
highfixed

409, never 404 stated twice without AD-26's Membership qualifier

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns, UJ-8
AD-26 permits 409 because "the caller holds a Membership in the Space, so AD-3's boundary rule does not apply" — the qualifier is load-bearing. Both spine statements dropped it and read as absolutes, so an implementer building the endpoint returns 409 for any offer id that is not Pending, including one in a Space the caller has no Membership in. The endpoint becomes a cross-Space existence oracle: 409 means the row exists somewhere in Yello, 404 means it does not.
Fix: both statements qualified, with the check order made explicit — resolve the Space context first, evaluate State = Pending second, never the reverse.
highfixed

Multi-tab never considered, and FR-34 pushes the implementer straight into it

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
One Account can hold two tabs on two different Spaces — not a cross-Space view, but a state neither spine named. An implementer chasing "observable without the participant acting" fans the notice out to every tab, then needs to disambiguate which Space ended, so the announcement becomes "Access to Northwind Redesign ended." — a Space name crossing a boundary through an assertive live region, spoken in a tab showing a different Space. Screen-reader users take the disclosure first and hardest, inverting the intent of the requirement.
Fix: delivery restricted to the client context whose active Space matches the change; cross-tab fan-out is a per-tab filter, never a shared announcement; the copy stays "Access ended." with no Space name in any case; and each tab holds its own Space context sharing no state.
highfixed

Inbound frames queued before lease invalidation had no specified disposal

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
FR-34 and AD-8 are thorough about the outbound direction. Nothing covered frames already delivered to the client and sitting in a render or announcement queue when the lease dies — so the polite Presence region announces "Ravi is editing", or a remote description edit renders, one tick after "Access ended.", for a Space the Account no longer belongs to. NFR-1's "a failure, timeout or partial response never discloses data or existence across a Space boundary" fails, on the requirement the PRD names as the criterion to judge the product on.
Fix: on lease invalidation the client discards every queued inbound frame for that Space and clears both live regions — ordered before the banner is announced, not after.
highfixed

The email-address rule from §6.1 appeared nowhere in either spine

EXPERIENCE.md
§6.1 and AD-23 confine addresses to Owners and Admins of a Space the Account is a Member of. The mechanism that breaks it is mundane and near-certain: 20px monospace initials and a display name do not disambiguate two people, and the field every implementer reaches for is the address. The word "email" appeared in EXPERIENCE.md only as an Invitation input and in a sign-in error.
Fix: a hard rule — no surface outside Space settings' Membership management renders an address; every other surface identifies a Membership by display name and initials; a Membership-scoped discriminator handles collisions; attribution renders the name captured at authoring time, never a live global lookup.
highfixed

Browser-owned surfaces carry Space names outside the app's control

EXPERIENCE.md § Isolation and Refusal
Four mechanisms, none mentioned: the delete-Space confirmation asks the user to type a Space name, feeding it into origin-scoped, Account-agnostic form autofill, so the next Account on that profile is offered another Account's Space name; document.title was unspecified, and an SPA setting "Northwind Redesign — Board" writes Space names into history, the OS window title, screen shares and synced history; the Invitation view is unauthenticated and renders a Space name, so its og: metadata is scraped by link-preview services into the chat logs of people with no Membership; and back/forward cache and scroll restoration are URL-keyed and browser-owned.
Fix: all four addressed — autocomplete="off" with non-reusable field names, a fixed document.title of "Yello", no Space name in the Invitation view's title or social metadata plus noindex, and scroll restoration disabled on Space-scoped routes.
highfixed

Three specified Account-scoped reads are richer than AD-24's "identity only"

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns, UJ-7
Naming every Space still owned when Account deletion is refused reads a Membership attribute at Account scope; stating the Spaces-per-Account bound requires an Account-scoped count on a surface AD-24 does not enumerate; and UJ-7 invited the Token list to render a per-Space Role. In all three the data is the caller's own, so NFR-1 is not breached — but AD-24 exists precisely to stop an implementer who needs an unauthorised read from disabling RLS or inventing a bypass that then spreads. Silence is what produces the bypass.
Fix: each decided explicitly — the first two permitted as own-data with AD-24 amendments flagged, and the Token→Role display dropped as a genuine Membership read across a boundary that was decoration rather than function.
highfixed

The Ownership Offer email ships with no disclosure constraint anywhere

EXPERIENCE.md UJ-8
It is the one genuinely new outbound artefact in the product, and NFR-1 explicitly binds notifications. UJ-2 does this correctly for the Invitation — "naming the Space, the Role, and who issued it — and nothing about the Space's contents". The offer email had no such clause, and because the PRD amendment is owed rather than made, no FR governs it either. An implementer writing "You've been offered ownership of Northwind Redesign (12 Projects, 340 Tasks, 8 Members)" would be adding helpful context with nothing telling them not to. Second-order: AD-5 lapses the offer when the recipient's Membership ends, but an email already sent cannot be recalled.
Fix: the same explicit clause added to UJ-8 step 2, and the owed PRD amendment now carries the disclosure constraint rather than only the notification trigger.
mediumfixed

Cold load renders the Space name before authorisation resolves

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
"The context bar can render before its contents" — but the name cannot come from a 404 (AD-3 strips it), so it can only come from cache. That makes the context bar distinguish "removed from" (name available locally) from "never existed" (no name), breaking the identical-in-every-respect rule; and on a shared browser profile it renders one Account's Space name to another. It also contradicts AD-1's "never inferred from a prior request".
Fix: the bar may render its shell, never its Space name, which is gated on the current request's Membership resolution.
mediumfixed

A boundary 404 in response to an optimistic write had no specified state

EXPERIENCE.md § Interaction Primitives
A correctness hole that pushes the implementer toward one of two rule violations: a silent revert, which breaches FR-34's observability and NFR-8's "degrade visibly, never silently", or a toast, which the Isolation section explicitly forbids.
Fix: it collapses to the same terminal treatment as any other boundary refusal — revert the replica, then render the full refusal surface. A final answer, not a retryable fault.
mediumfixed

Nothing forbade the client requesting a Space id it had not just been told it belongs to

EXPERIENCE.md § Isolation and Refusal
No data leaks — this degrades the detection mechanism the release gate depends on. Warm-start prefetching of a remembered last-Space produces a boundary 404 and, per AD-20, an AccessRefusal classified CrossSpace. NFR-7 exists so cross-Space attempts are distinguishable from within-Space failures; a client routinely manufacturing them for its own ordinary users buries a real probing attack in its own noise.
Fix: the client never issues a Space-scoped request for a Space id absent from the current switcher response — one rule closing the warm-start hole and the deep-link-after-removal hole together.
mediumfixed

No state for an unrecognised or forged Invitation token

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
Only "revoked" was specified, so an implementer renders something else — a generic 404, "Not available.", "Invalid link" — for a token that never existed, and any difference makes the acceptance route an existence oracle. Guid entropy makes exploitation impractical, which is why it is medium; but it is AD-23's uniformity discipline applied one surface over, and nearly free.
Fix: one row covering revoked, accepted, expired, lapsed and unrecognised with a single response — identical words, shape and duration.
lowfixed

Presence announcement content unspecified; FR-32's cross-Space clause not restated

EXPERIENCE.md §§ Accessibility Floor, Component Patterns
The display-name case is fine — Presence shows only same-Space Memberships and roster names are already visible to every Role. But nothing said the announcement could not carry an address. Several browsers' enhanced spellcheck and several mobile IMEs transmit typed text to a remote service, and the description editor is the product's largest free-text surface. Outside NFR-1's Space-to-Space framing but inside §6.1's spirit and NFR-6's "no content in any log".
