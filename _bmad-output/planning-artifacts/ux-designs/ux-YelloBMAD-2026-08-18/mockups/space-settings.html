Key screen — Space settings

Three rules live here and nowhere else in the product, which is why this surface earned a mock. Email addresses appear on this screen only — §6.1 confines them to Owners and Admins of a Space the Account is a Member of, and every other surface identifies a Membership by display name and initials. FR-13's Role narrowing is visible as absence, not as disabled controls. And the Owner's row carries no remove control for anyone, including the Owner (FR-14).

Two details that are requirements. Beatriz's row shows EDITING NOW — removing her interrupts a live session mid-sentence (UJ-6), and the remover should know that before they click. And the Space name field carries autocomplete="off": form autofill is origin-scoped and Account-agnostic, so without it the next Account on this browser profile is offered another Account's Space name. FR-14 · §6.1

Ravi has no remove control on his own row. The Owner's Membership cannot be removed by anyone, including the Owner, while it holds ownership — he leaves by having an offer accepted, or by deleting the Space. The pending offer above is not itself an exit.

Four differences, every one an absence. The Owner's row has no Role picker. Tomás's own row shows his Role as a fact, not a control — Admins cannot modify each other. There is no Ownership panel and no delete button at all, rather than greyed-out ones. And the Role pickers he does have offer Member and Viewer only. Nothing on this screen tells him what he cannot do; the context bar's accessible name does that on arrival.

He can still revoke the Invitation Ravi issued — FR-12 makes any Owner or Admin able to revoke any pending Invitation, including one whose issuer has since been demoted, removed or deleted.

3 · Deleting the Space

The most destructive operation in the product and the only one with no undo. §6.2 requires that this is stated at the point of the action.

yello.app/s/northwind/settings — delete
Northwind Redesign▾Space settingsOWNER

Delete Northwind Redesign?

This cannot be undone.

You will destroy:

  • 5 Projects and 1,284 Tasks
  • 3 other people's access to all of it
  • 2 pending invitations and every API Token for this Space

Friction scaled to blast radius, which is the mitigation the UX owes for keeping deletion irreversible. It names the object, states irreversibility in four words, counts what goes, and says plainly that other people lose access — the consequence an Owner is least likely to have in mind. The copy carries the destructiveness; the danger border only reinforces it, because accent and danger sit 1.19:1 apart and converge under deuteranopia.
