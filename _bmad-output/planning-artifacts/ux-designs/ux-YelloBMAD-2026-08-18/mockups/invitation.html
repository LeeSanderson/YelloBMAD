Key screen — the Invitation view

The only page an unauthenticated stranger ever sees, and Beatriz's first — possibly only — impression of Yello. It carries the most generous padding in the product; this is the one place the engineered register is deliberately loosened.

Three of its rules are counter-intuitive enough to be worth seeing rendered: presenting the Invitation is a side-effect-free read (AD-28), so loading this page creates nothing; all five terminal states share one response, including a token that never existed; and the wrong-Account case must refuse without echoing either address.

Page metadata is part of the spec here, not an afterthought. This page legitimately renders a Space name — FR-11 requires the Space and Role be shown before acceptance — so the leak is not the page, it is what the page emits: <title>Yello</title> with no Space name, no og: or twitter: metadata, and noindex. Paste this link into Slack without that and a link-preview service scrapes the Space name into the chat logs of people with no Membership.

Nothing has happened yet on the left. No Membership, no Account, no state change — a mail security scanner, a link prefetcher or a colleague the mail was forwarded to all produce exactly the same nothing. Acceptance is the separate, explicit act on the right. AD-28 · FR-11

The in-flight state matters more here than anywhere else in Yello, because this is one of only two submit-and-wait interactions in a product that otherwise has no save button — and AD-23 makes it deliberately slow, performing the password hash even for an address already on file so the response cannot be timed. Without a stated in-flight state, the slowest interaction in the product looks broken.

The footer line is the whole of UJ-3's resolution in eight words: she becomes Owner of a Personal Space she may never use, and it does not compete for her attention.

The left-hand card is the same for five different causes, and that uniformity is the point. It names no Space, no contents, and nobody who revoked it. Critically it is also identical for a token that never existed — a distinct response there would make the acceptance route an existence oracle, which is AD-23's registration discipline applied one surface over. Guid entropy makes exploiting it impractical, which is why the rule is cheap rather than urgent — but it is nearly free to get right and expensive to retrofit.

The right-hand card names neither address. The helpful version — “this is for nadia@studio.co, you're signed in as ravi@anand.dev” — discloses the invited address to whoever holds the link, and on a forwarded link that is a stranger. §6.1 confines addresses to Owners and Admins of a Space the Account is already a Member of, so an unauthenticated page is the last place one may appear.
