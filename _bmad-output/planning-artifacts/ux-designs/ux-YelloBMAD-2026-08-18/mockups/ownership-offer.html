Key screen — Ownership Offer

The surface with no precedent in any product, and the decision that unblocked information architecture. A Member or Viewer named in an Ownership Offer previously had nowhere at all to see it: FR-8 lets any Role be named, AD-26 authorises acceptance by row identity rather than Role, and PRD §7 scopes Space settings to Owner and Admin.

The obvious answer — badge the Space in the switcher so Nadia sees it without going in — is illegal. AD-24 permits that surface to return Space identity only, and AD-26 rules out an offers inbox explicitly. So the indicator lives on the Space context bar: already present for every Role, already Space-scoped, and already the thing that answers “what is my standing here?”

1 · The indicator — Nadia, acting as a Member

Accent appears in chrome exactly once in the whole product, and this is it — because it is the only chrome element that is a proposition rather than a statement. Note she is a MEMBER: the affordance reaches her because acceptance is authorised by row identity, not by the FR-16 Role matrix.

It is an indicator only. The decision opens in a dialog with room to explain, because what she is agreeing to is genuinely consequential and cannot be conveyed in a chrome-sized affordance.

2 · The decision

This is one of only two places the terse voice is deliberately overridden. FR-42 binds the new Owner immediately — her Membership cannot be removed while she holds ownership, and her Account deletion is refused until she transfers the Space onward or deletes it. Nobody should agree to that from a one-line dialog.

Ravi Anand has offered you ownership of Northwind Redesign.

Accepting makes you the Owner. Ravi becomes an Admin and keeps his access.

As Owner you would take on three things that cannot be undone by leaving:

  • You could not remove your own Membership while you hold ownership.
  • You could not delete your Yello Account until you pass ownership on, or delete this Space.
  • Passing it on needs someone else to accept. Nobody can be made an Owner without agreeing.

What Owners can do

Accept and Decline are both explicit and neither is the default — there is no pre-focused primary, because a mis-hit Enter should not transfer a Space. The link is underlined, not accent-coloured alone: accent against body text is only 2.66:1, below what WCAG 1.4.1 requires when colour is the sole cue.

Expiry is only visible here because of an upstream change. FR-8 originally surfaced the offer in-Space and explicitly not by email, with a 7-day expiry evaluated on read. Composed, Nadia was never told and had to happen to walk in within a week — and UJ-8’s own edge case then traps Ravi as Owner indefinitely. Being assigned a Task emails you (FR-40); being offered an entire Space did not. Emailing the offer is a pending PRD amendment.

3 · No longer open

A designed state, not an error toast — she may have had the dialog open for a while, and the offer may have been revoked, declined elsewhere, lapsed, or lost to a concurrent offer hitting the filtered unique index.

The indicator has already gone from the context bar — it is present only while the offer is Pending. Note what the copy does not say: not which of the five reasons applied, and not who acted. And the status code is Role-dependent: 409 is correct for Nadia because she holds a Membership, so AD-3’s boundary rule does not apply to her. A caller with no Membership must get 404 on the same route — 409 would confirm that the Space, and an offer inside it, exist.
